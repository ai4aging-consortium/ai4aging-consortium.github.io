A scientific consortium for AI in aging and longevity research

The AI for Aging Consortium brings together leading groups in artificial intelligence and aging research. Its members write shared guidelines for how AI is built, evaluated and reported in the field, and build resources that any laboratory can use, so that aging and longevity research moves faster and its findings hold up. Everything the consortium produces is released openly.

Vision

Over the next ten years the consortium’s members will agree on how AI should be built, evaluated and reported in aging research, build the datasets and models the field needs, test AI agents on aging research tasks, and use these resources to shorten the path from data to tested interventions. The work spans human cohorts, clinical studies, cells, model organisms and intervention studies.

Aging research is slow for reasons that AI could help with. Outcomes arrive years or decades after the measurements meant to predict them, data sit in separate laboratories in formats that do not match, and published models are hard to rerun on new data. Each group also decides for itself how to test and report its AI, so results are hard to compare. No single laboratory can fix these problems, but leading groups that agree on standards and share their data and tools with the whole field can.

How the consortium works

Leading groups in AI and aging research work within shared guidelines to build resources for the whole field, with help from open contributors.

  1. Leading groups that build AI systems and leading groups that study aging join the consortium by invitation. Any researcher or data holder can contribute and is credited for the work.
  2. Members agree on guidelines for how AI is built, evaluated and reported in aging research.
  3. Members and contributors build resources that any laboratory can use, starting with curated datasets and open models, and test existing AI agents on aging research tasks.
  4. Members also build a benchmark and an open index of AI systems, so that anyone can see what each system has been shown to do.
  5. White papers draw on evidence from every resource. They carry the guidelines and the field’s shared view of what has been shown and what to build next.
  6. Everything the consortium produces is released openly, so that aging and longevity research can become faster and more reliable.

Seven pillars

Each pillar appears in the diagram above.

Consortium
Leading groups in artificial intelligence and aging research join by invitation. Open contributors are credited for logged contributions.
Guidelines
Agreed rules for how AI studies of aging are built, evaluated and reported, starting with a reporting checklist and a model card.
Open datasets
Public aging data reprocessed and labeled for AI, with documented error rates.
Open models
Published aging models packaged to run on new data in a common format, each with a model card and baselines scored before release.
AI agents
Existing AI agents tested on aging research tasks, each with a public card of its results. The consortium does not build its own agents.
Benchmark
Versioned tasks for comparing AI systems on the same data, scored by the consortium with public scoring code. Sealed tasks are hosted by the consortium.
White papers
An overview of the field and four theme papers that carry the guidelines and the field’s shared view. Each releases an open resource, starting with an open index of AI systems for aging.

White papers

The white papers carry the consortium’s guidelines and the field’s shared view. The series will review the evidence for AI in aging research and set priorities for what to build, and each paper will deposit an open resource with a DOI before the journal version appears.

  1. Overview

    A roadmap for artificial intelligence in aging research

    Which aging data exist, which are missing, and which kinds of model have been tested externally, prospectively or in a randomized trial.

  2. Data

    Data standards for artificial intelligence in aging research

    How aging data should be prepared for AI, and where deep molecular profiles and long follow-up exist in the same people.

  3. Models

    Recommendations for building and validating AI models of aging

    Whether risk predictions hold up in external and prospective tests, and what that requires of the models.

  4. Agents

    Guidelines for AI scientists in aging research

    How far interventions nominated by AI agents get toward a measured outcome, and what each agent should report.

  5. Evaluation

    Reporting and evaluation guidelines for AI in aging research

    What evidence each use of a learned aging measure requires, and how studies should report it.

How the consortium runs

Open outputs. Everything the consortium produces is released openly, including guidelines, white papers, datasets, models, tools, the open index and benchmark results. Each white paper will release its resource before the journal version.

Members by invitation. Members are leading groups in artificial intelligence and aging research. The Founding Scientific Committee nominates and approves new members, and committee terms rotate. Members sign the charter and declare their interests.

Open to contributors. Researchers and data holders outside the membership can contribute data, benchmark tasks, models and sections of the white papers. Each contribution is logged and credited.

Volunteer-run. Every role in the consortium is unpaid.

Open scoring. The consortium scores the benchmark with public code. A system is not scored on a task whose sealed data its developers have seen, and systems from the Founding Director’s laboratory or a sponsor are labeled as affiliated.

The Founding Scientific Committee will be announced.
